01 / Overview

Small contacts.
Useful feedback.

Weak contact can be hidden by friction, motion, and noise in a robot’s built-in signals. Contact-free Proprioceptive Response Estimation (CoPRE) learns the torque expected during free motion, making contact-induced deviations easier to detect.

Training and calibration use only contact-free recordings. On ARX L5 and the G1 right arm, CoPRE improves contact detection using proprioception; ARX demonstrations use that feedback for protective retreat and insertion retries.

02 / Method

Predict nominal torque.
Detect contact-induced changes.

Two complementary designs: state exclusion and noise-weighted residual aggregation.

  1. 01

    Learn free motion

    Train on contact-free state and command logs. Calibrate the detection threshold using separate contact-free recordings.

  2. 02

    Predict from an earlier state

    Use earlier state–command history and known commands to predict nominal torque. Withhold state observations inside the prediction interval so recent contact cannot alter that reference.

  3. 03

    Weight and combine residuals

    Remove nominal bias and combine torque residuals through a noise-weighted Jacobian. Three consecutive samples at or above the calibrated threshold confirm contact.

Combined calibration; shared comparison rules within each robot. ARX evaluates detection through push end; G1 uses a 0.5 s window. Reference resistance is measured separately at sliding onset; the 90% criterion must hold at that and all higher tested levels.

Evaluation protocol and metric definitions

ARX selects the lowest threshold with zero confirmed calibration alarms. G1 permits at most 1% alarm time in each calibration motion set and uses three-fold cross-fitting. Training and calibration are separate for each robot.

Learned-model recall averages three seeds; dynamics is evaluated once. The 95% confidence intervals bootstrap physical trials, grouping seed evaluations. The evaluation window begins at a geometric contact reference, not independently measured contact onset.

Broad-motion alarm time is the fraction of held-out broad contact-free motion spent in confirmed alarm. Reference resistance characterizes the pushing condition, not force at detection. CoPRE’s score is used for contact detection, not validated force measurement.

04 / Manipulation

Use contact to guide
the next attempt.

Qualitative ARX demonstrations connect contact detection to protective retreat and belief-guided retries.

Protective retreat during placement

When a cup blocks placement of a carried block, detected contact triggers a stop and retreat. The robot places the block at an alternate location, leaving the cup upright.

Contact-guided book insertion

Contact with a neighboring book blocks the first attempt. Feedback updates a spatial belief map and guides pose adjustments and retries until the book enters the gap.

05 / Further evaluation

Testing the design choices.

Matched ablations examine prediction, residual scoring, and temporal design on the same benchmark recordings.

State exclusion

Withholding observations within the prediction interval improves contact recall on both platforms.

Noise-weighted scoring

Weighting residuals by joint variability preserves sensitivity under broader contact-free calibration.
