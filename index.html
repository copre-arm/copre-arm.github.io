01 / Overview

Small contacts.
Useful feedback.

Robots use contact feedback to stop when blocked and adjust their next motion. On low-cost arms, contact-induced joint torque changes can be small relative to normal motion variations and noise. We call these interactions weak contact.

Contact-free Proprioceptive Response Estimation (CoPRE) learns the joint torque expected during contact-free motion from built-in joint states and motor signals. Training and calibration use only contact-free recordings, without additional force sensors or force labels.

Two designs preserve sensitivity: the predictor withholds state observations within the prediction interval, and noise-weighted Jacobian aggregation combines the torque residuals into a contact score. Experiments evaluate both designs on the ARX L5 and the G1 right arm.

02 / Method

Predict nominal torque.
Detect contact-induced changes.

Keep contact-induced changes in the residual and account for each joint’s variability during contact-free motion.

  1. 01

    Learn free motion

    Train on contact-free state and command logs. Calibrate the detection threshold using separate contact-free recordings.

  2. 02

    Predict from an earlier state

    Use earlier state–command history and known commands to predict nominal torque. Withhold state observations inside the prediction interval so recent contact cannot alter that reference.

  3. 03

    Weight and combine residuals

    Remove nominal bias and combine torque residuals through a noise-weighted Jacobian. Three consecutive samples above the calibrated threshold confirm contact.

03 / Experiments

Evaluated on two
robot platforms.

Each arm pushes five book stacks at three speeds, with three repetitions per condition: 45 physical trials per robot.

ARX L5 · Six-joint arm

74.1% overall trial recall

Both the adapted neural and nominal dynamics baselines detect 0% of trials under combined calibration.

3.5 N

3.32%

Unitree G1 · Seven-joint right arm

82.2% overall trial recall

The adapted neural baseline reaches 16.3% recall and the nominal dynamics baseline reaches 42.2%.

Reference resistance at 90% detection
5.5 N
Held-out broad-motion alarm time
0.86%

Comparisons use shared calibration rules within each robot. Combined calibration includes task-matched and broader contact-free motion: ARX permits zero confirmed calibration alarms; G1 uses a 1% alarm-time budget in each calibration motion set. The two platforms use separate training and calibration.

Recall is averaged over three model seeds. Detection is evaluated from a geometric contact reference through push end on ARX, and within 0.5 s on G1. Reference resistances are measured separately at sliding onset. The reported 90% criterion must also hold at all higher tested levels; these values are not forces measured at the instant of detection.

Matched ablations support state exclusion and noise-weighted aggregation on both robots. The transformer is a shared benchmark configuration; a GRU achieves better G1 results in the predictor-family ablation.

04 / Manipulation

Use contact to guide
the next attempt.

Qualitative ARX demonstrations connect contact detection to protective retreat and belief-guided retries.

Protective retreat during placement

When a cup blocks placement of a carried block, detected contact triggers a stop and retreat. The robot places the block at an alternate location, leaving the cup upright.

Contact-guided book insertion

Contact with a neighboring book blocks the first attempt. Feedback updates a spatial belief map and guides an adjusted insertion pose; the next attempt succeeds.
