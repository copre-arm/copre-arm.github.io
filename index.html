01 / Overview

Small contacts.
Useful feedback.

Low-cost robot arms already measure their own motion and actuator response. These signals offer a way to sense physical interaction without adding dedicated force sensors. But weak contact can be hidden by the torque variations of ordinary motion.

Contact-free Proprioceptive Response Estimation (CoPRE) learns how a robot responds during contact-free motion. It predicts the current response from an earlier state history and known commands, then compares predicted and measured joint torque to obtain evidence of contact.

The key is when the predictor looks: measurements inside the interval being tested are reserved for detection, so they cannot alter the nominal prediction for that interval.

02 / Method

Predict the motion.
Listen to the difference.

Separate the information used for prediction from the response used as contact evidence.

  1. 01

    Learn free motion

    Train on contact-free state and command logs. Calibrate the detection threshold using separate contact-free recordings.

  2. 02

    Predict from an earlier state

    Use a pre-interval history and known commands to predict the response, withholding state measurements within the tested interval.

  3. 03

    Turn residuals into feedback

    Compare measured and predicted torque. Convert the residual into contact evidence that can inform the robot’s next action.
